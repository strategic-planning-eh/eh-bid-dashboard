## What moved since August

Since the August review the Vision 2030 portfolio has kept splitting into two tiers. The commercial tier advanced: NEOM Green Hydrogen finished construction and entered commissioning ($8.5bn, operations 2027); Oxagon's $5bn AI data-centre campus broke ground in September and signed Nebius as anchor tenant in October, with a 400km freight-rail link and wastewater plant out to tender; Red Sea Global reached 12 operating resorts with the Grand Hyatt opening on 31 October; Diriyah's awards passed SAR 130bn; SABIC swung back to profit in Q3 and took FID on a new ammonia-urea complex; SPARK landed a $2.1bn Dalipal lease; and Aramco (3 Nov) kept Jafurah phase two on track. The prestige tier kept shrinking at the edges: Reuters confirmed the NEOM stadium inside The Line is shelved indefinitely, Semafor (uncorroborated) reported five 2034 World Cup stadiums to be dropped (NEOM, KAEC, Qiddiya Coast, Roshn, New Murabba), the Mukaab foundation award due in October has not surfaced, Lucid's AMP-2 will not contribute to 2026 volumes after deep cuts and a $750m PIF top-up, and NEOM's container terminal slipped to a 2027 ramp-up. The fiscal backdrop hardened — the 30 Sep pre-budget statement cut 2027 spending ~3%, revised the 2026 deficit to SAR 245bn and said debt will rise to protect "priority projects" — while the Red Sea war became a live operating risk rather than a background one (Houthi blockade on Saudi shipping from 20 Jul, East-West pipeline struck 10 Sep, Bab el-Mandeb flows halved in September), which bears most directly on King Abdullah Port, Port of NEOM and Red Sea Global. Net: three field changes (NGHC status/investment, King Salman Park completion/investment, Lucid completion), two confidence moves each way, and twelve records with nothing new in the window — a portfolio that is executing where cash flow exists and quietly deferring where it does not.
