1. How big the fund is, 2018–2025

"Fund size" here means what PIF calls assets under management: the total value of everything it owns and invests on behalf of the state. The dashed line is the SAR 4 trillion size PIF set itself as a target for the end of 2025. From 2022 the headline figure also includes money PIF is owed by others; the second button removes that. 2. Where the money sits: PIF's eight "pools"

From 2021 to 2025 PIF grouped its money into eight buckets it calls pools: four for Saudi Arabia, three for investments abroad, and one for cash kept ready. Each colour below is one bucket. The two biggest, in green and purple, are stakes in listed Saudi companies (such as Aramco and the banks) and the new Saudi companies PIF has founded itself. From 2026 PIF is replacing these buckets with three "portfolios".

Each year uses the most recent report that shows it: 2020 from the 2022 report (the 2021 report only named the buckets), 2021 from the 2023 report, 2022 from the 2024 report, and 2023–2025 from the 2025 report. Where an earlier report gave a different figure it is listed in section 9.

5. The accounts, 2020–2025

Each report ends with three summary tables of accounts. They add together PIF and every company it controls, banks included, which is why the totals here are bigger than the fund size in section 1. What PIF owns, what it has borrowed, and what is left

6. Green bonds

A green bond is money PIF borrows on the promise of spending it only on environmental projects. PIF lists seven allowed types, including "sustainable water management" and "pollution prevention and control". Bars: total borrowed so far and how much of it has actually been assigned to projects, in billions of dollars. Line: the number of projects funded.

What PIF says the money will achieve

The 2023 numbers describe every project that could qualify; the 2025 numbers describe only the projects that have been given money. They measure different things, so the drop from 49.4 to 4 million cubic metres is not a fall in activity.

10. What the five reports show

This part only repeats what the reports say. Our own reading is in the blue box at the end.

Improved every year

Everything PIF owns on the accounts basis (SAR 2.06 trillion in 2020 to SAR 4.54 trillion in 2025), borrowings (SAR 197 bn to SAR 725 bn), staff numbers (1,457 to 3,321), the total raised through green bonds, and the number of green projects funded (35, then 91, then 146).

Reversed in 2025

The fund size fell for the first time in the series, from SAR 3,434 bn to SAR 3,396 bn, and the SAR 4 trillion target was missed by 15%. What is left after debts fell 2%. The average yearly return since 2017 has gone down every year it has been reported the same way: 8% (2022), 8.7% (2023), 7.2% (2024), 5.8% (2025). The two biggest Saudi buckets, listed company stakes and PIF-founded companies, both shrank. The number of companies PIF owns fell from 225 to 211. New hires fell for a second year (812, 614, 549).

Disclosed earlier but dropped later

A single-year return (25% in 2021; never given again). A return for each bucket (the 2023 report gave 19.4% a year for PIF-founded companies and 10.8% for listed stakes since 2017; the 2024 and 2025 reports do not). The share of the fund in "climate solutions" and "climate enablers" (2% and 21% in the 2024 report; gone in 2025). Jobs created (77,700 in 2021, 181,000 in 2022, 730,249 cumulative in 2023; nothing in 2024 or 2025). Companies founded in the year (given 2021–2024, not 2025). PIF's own 13-sector table (2021 only).

Added later

Bucket values by year (from 2022), the standard industry split (from 2023), a cash-flow table (from 2023), a separate cash share (from 2022), local supplier history (2025 only), the economic contribution actually achieved (from 2024), Private Sector Hub counts (2025), and the 2026–2030 structure with its named "Clean Energy, Water and Renewables Infrastructure" ecosystem (2025).

Our sector specifically

Across five reports, waste and recycling content amounts to: one line on SIRC's marine operations (2022), reverse vending machine counts (2024 and 2025), Qiddiya's construction-site figures (2025), a PIF corporate waste strategy (2024–2025), and a passing reference at Red Sea Global (2024). Water appears through ACWA desalination every year, through a NEOM/Veolia desalination plant (2022), through green bond wastewater figures (2023, 2025) and, in 2025, through the name of a strategic ecosystem. SIRC's own size, assets or plans are never disclosed in any of the five reports.

Analysis (our reading, not PIF's statement)

The series tells a clear story. The fund grew fast because the government handed it assets (the 4% Aramco stake in 2022 alone added SAR 296 bn) and because it borrowed. Its return has been falling since the reports first measured it consistently. 2025 is the first year the headline size went backwards, and it is also the year of the biggest jump in borrowing. That is why the 2026–2030 language turns to "value realization" and getting the private sector to take on more of the work.

Energy's share has risen from 23% to roughly 28–32% since 2023, while the "unclassified" slice has shrunk. Utilities has stayed at 4–6%. Nothing in PIF's industry list tracks environmental services, so PIF's own reporting cannot tell you whether its involvement in our sector is growing.

The sustainability sections have grown more than sevenfold since 2021 ("sustainab-" appears 21 times in the 2021 report and 158 times in 2025) while waste content has gone from nothing to a handful of sentences. Most of the growth is description of policies and awards; the reverse vending machines and Qiddiya's 82% figure are the only operating numbers. Read that as: PIF reports sustainability as a way of managing companies, not as a set of contracts.

The one place the reports point to real future demand is water. It is the only environmental theme that appears in every year's operating content, and in 2025 it was written into a named strategic ecosystem. If the 2026 report gives that ecosystem a value, water will have become a tracked line; waste has not yet reached that stage.

Two cautions on the comparison itself. First, PIF quietly revises earlier years in later reports (fund size, bucket values, cash flows). This dashboard takes the latest figure every time, so it will not match a series copied from the original reports. Second, the green bond impact figures changed what they measure between 2023 and 2025; the drop from 49.4 to 4 million cubic metres of wastewater is a change of definition, not of activity.
