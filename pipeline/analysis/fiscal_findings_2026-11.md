## 4. War and shipping

**4.1 Bab el-Mandeb / Red Sea status**
- 5–6 Oct: Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces ("Dawn of Yemen" offensive, with Saudi air strikes) retook Dhubab and positions around Bab el-Mandeb and reached/entered Mocha; Houthis called the claims fabricated. Saudi naval strikes on Hodeidah hit explosive-boat and mine stores (Al Jazeera, 6 Oct; Al-Monitor, Oct). 27 Oct: Houthis said they repelled a Saudi-backed advance near the strait (Reuters/Al-Monitor, 27 Oct). Net: the coast around the strait is contested; the Houthis no longer hold it uncontested as they did on 11/09, but Hodeidah and the highlands remain Houthi.
- Houthi shipping embargo on Saudi-linked vessels (declared 20 Jul) remains in force; AFP on 17 Oct reported the Houthis had blockaded Bab el-Mandeb to Saudi-flagged tankers; WSJ (cited 7 Nov) says some vessels request safe passage by email. Last corroborated Houthi strike on a ship was 24 Aug (ACLED, 1 Oct); a small-boat attack on an LNG tanker off Mukalla on 17 Oct. UKMTO: 23 attacks on ships region-wide since 1 Oct (as of 19 Oct), most in Hormuz.
- Mainline container carriers are returning to Suez (Maersk 67 transits in Sep vs 8 a year earlier; Gemini AE12 via Suez from 27 Oct) under naval protection; war-risk premiums for Saudi ports north of Jizan (Yanbu, Jeddah) about 1% of hull value. Suez Canal Aug transits 1,358 (+27% y/y).
- Sources: https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/6/yemen-war-whats-the-latest-on-the-battlefield (6 Oct 2026); https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/10/yemens-houthis-say-they-hit-saudi-ras-tanura-oil-refinery-missile (27 Oct 2026); https://acleddata.com/report/houthi-gains-around-bab-al-mandab-raise-risks-red-sea-shipping-and-global-oil-markets (1 Oct 2026); https://www.forwardernews.com/news/2026-10-18-red-sea-houthis-and-egypt-s-saudi-vote (19 Oct 2026); https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/middle-east-war-update-qeshm-island-strike-raises-tensions-talks-continue-1.500739148 (8 Nov 2026).
- What changes on the page: Section 06 narrative ("Houthis took Mokha, Perim and the rest of the coast") must be updated: government forces retook Dhubab/Mocha area in early October; strait contested; Houthi embargo on Saudi ships still declared but not enforced by strikes since August; Yanbu exports running ~2.5 mb/d. Section 07 "War exposure — High" for Red Sea projects: still high but note shipping lines returning to Suez.

**4.2 Attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure, October–November**
- 1 Oct: projectile struck inside Yanbu port, fire, loading of a tanker briefly halted then resumed (Forwarder News, 19 Oct). 4–5 Oct: Houthis claimed strikes on an Aramco refinery at Rabigh, Riyadh airport and Abha/Jazan/Najran airports; Saudi confirmed only Jazan and Najran (three minor injuries) (Al Jazeera, 6 Oct). 17 Oct: coalition intercepted a ballistic missile aimed at Yanbu plus two cruise missiles and three drones; same day a tanker was hit by an RPG 78 nm NE of Jubail (Al Jazeera 17 Oct; Forwarder News). 27 Oct: Houthis claimed a ballistic-missile strike on Ras Tanura refinery/terminal (550 kb/d); Bloomberg: a fire near the Ras Tanura terminal was contained; Aramco/Saudi did not confirm (Al-Monitor/Reuters, 27 Oct). 29–30 Oct: Houthi claims against Ras Tanura, Rabigh and Abqaiq; cruise missile intercepted (Nation Thailand, 31 Oct). 6–7 Nov: drones intercepted near Riyadh and Mecca; cruise missile toward Riyadh intercepted 7 Nov; Houthis claimed six operations against Aramco facilities and Riyadh airport (7 Nov); ballistic missile toward the Eastern Region intercepted 8 Nov (Gulf News live, 8 Nov). Aramco (3 Nov): facilities attacked in Q3 "and afterward"; impact not material.
- East–West pipeline: restarted 22 Sep at reduced throughput (Gulf News, 31 Oct); Aramco says it provided export flexibility and western loadings were restored quickly; no new outage reported in October–November (searched; none found). Yanbu/Red Sea exports ~2.5 mb/d in October (Vortexa).
- What changes on the page: Section 06 "What happened" paragraph — add the October–November attack list; Section 08 point 3 (spill readiness on the Red Sea coast) now extends to the Gulf coast (Ras Tanura fire 27/10, Jubail RPG attack 17/10) — recommend EH confirm readiness for Jubail–Ras Tanura–Abqaiq as well.

**4.3 Hormuz, US–Iran and diplomacy**
- Hormuz: 12+ ships attacked in the week to 16 Oct (UKMTO), most since the war began 28 Feb; flows 11.6 mb/d in week to 13 Oct (68% of 17.1 mb/d pre-war baseline, Kpler); CENTCOM claims 400 ships / 100 m bbl through the strait in a week under the US "shuttle" with Arab partners; US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues ("ironclad"; US says it has cost Iran US$25 bn). 30 Oct limpet-mine strike on a tanker; Iran reports supertanker fires from mines 17 and 30 Oct. 6–7 Nov: US "limited" strikes on Iranian air defences on Qeshm Island; Iran claims a US MQ-9 downed; a tanker hit mines east of Hormuz 7 Nov; US disabled a cargo ship breaching the blockade 7 Nov.

Saudi–Houthi: no truce; Oman channel reported in September; coalition air strikes continue (Taiz market strike 6 Nov, 30+ killed per Houthis).

## Plain-English summary — what happened since 1 October (for senior management)
The Ministry of Finance's July–September report, published on 3 November, shows the government's deficit for the first nine months of 2026 at SAR 244 billion — already the whole amount the Ministry had said the full year would cost. The quarter's gap was SAR 84 billion, mainly because oil income fell 23% from a year earlier: prices stayed high (Brent about US$91 on average) but Saudi Arabia was shipping far less oil during the summer. Spending actually fell 4% year on year in the quarter, the first sign of real restraint since the war began, while the cost of servicing debt rose sharply (+38% over nine months) as public debt reached SAR 1,774 billion at end-September, up 17% since January; reserves at the central bank were unchanged at about SAR 400 billion. Since then the picture has improved on the export side: Saudi oil exports recovered to near pre-war volumes in October through US-escorted Gulf shipping and about 2.5 million barrels a day from Yanbu, Aramco reported strong Q3 profits and said it will maximise output in the final quarter, and OPEC+ kept Saudi Arabia's quota at 10.5 million barrels a day. Brent averaged roughly US$108 in October and peaked near US$113–118 on 30 October before easing to about US$100–103 in early November as US–Iran mediation showed progress. The war itself has not ended: Yemeni government forces pushed the Houthis back from the Bab el-Mandeb coast in early October, but the Houthis still declare an embargo on Saudi ships and have claimed missile and drone attacks on Ras Tanura, Rabigh, Abqaiq and Riyadh through 7 November, and the US carried out limited strikes on Iran on 6–7 November while talks continued. The IMF now expects the Saudi economy to shrink 3.3% this year before a strong rebound in 2027, and official statistics show GDP down 3.8% year on year in the third quarter. No official decisions on Vision 2030 projects, the 2027 budget date, taxes or fees were published in the period; the next tests are the OPEC+ meeting on 29 November, the 2027 budget in December and the Ministry's 2027 borrowing plan.
