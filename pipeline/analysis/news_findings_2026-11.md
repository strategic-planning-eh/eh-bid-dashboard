**C01**
- entity: Saudi Aramco · group: Energy · date: 03/11/2026
- headline: Aramco's third-quarter profit rose 9% to $31.2 billion and, for the first time this year, its spare cash fully covered the dividend.
- detail: Aramco published its July–September results on 3 November. Adjusted net income was $31.2 billion, up from $28.6 billion a year earlier, helped by refining margins that lifted downstream earnings more than threefold. Cash from operations was $38.7 billion and free cash flow $28.2 billion — comfortably above the $21.9 billion base dividend, which rose 3.5% and is payable in the fourth quarter. Borrowing fell: gearing dropped to 5.5% from 6.2% at end-June. Operationally, production started at the Zuluf crude increment, and the company said Jafurah phase two, the Fadhili gas expansion and the Dammam project are on track. The chief executive said attacks on energy facilities continued to amplify the global supply crisis, and the company is exploring extra overseas storage and new export routes alongside the East-West pipeline.
- financials: Adjusted net income $31.2bn (+9%); operating cash flow $38.7bn; free cash flow $28.2bn vs $21.9bn dividend; gearing 5.5%.
- partners: Saudi government (main shareholder); PIF (HUMAIN co-owner); Sinopec (Yanbu refinery partner).
- implications: The cash squeeze flagged in the last two editions has eased for now — but only because prices stayed high while volumes were constrained; the budget still shows the strain (see G02).

- headline: A Houthi missile wave hit Aramco's Dhahran headquarters and started a fire, while the Jazan refinery stayed shut three months after it was first struck.
- detail: On 13 October Houthi missiles targeted several Saudi cities including Riyadh and Dhahran. Eleven days later, on 24 October, the Houthis claimed seven operations against eight sites including Aramco facilities at Yanbu and the Rabigh refinery; Saudi authorities confirmed intercepting two ballistic missiles heading for Yanbu and Taif and issued civil-defence alerts for Makkah, Jeddah, Taif and Yanbu, but reported no verified damage at the named facilities.

- partners: Saudi-led coalition; Saudi Civil Defence; Aramco.
- implications: The first strike on Aramco's corporate heart shows nothing is out of range; for the west coast, the 24 October alerts for Jeddah, Rabigh and Yanbu are the closest the war has come to EH's own operating area.

## PART C

### Items to watch — November–December 2026

1. **11 November — CMA deadline for nine-month results.** Ma'aden, Alujain (NATPET, Yanbu), Saudi Electricity, Luberef (used oil) and Sadara's shareholders report; look for any Jubail restart language from SABIC affiliates (Yansab, Kayan, Petrokemya) and for Sadara's first word on its debt.
2. **29 November — OPEC+ monthly meeting, then Aramco's December price for Asia (~5 December).** A move back toward parity would mark the end of the market-share phase; another deep discount means export volumes are still the binding constraint.
3. **Early/mid-December — 2027 Budget Statement.** Pre-budget says SAR 1,392bn spend / SAR 190bn deficit; watch MEWA, NCEC and municipal allocations and any new fees or environmental levies.
4. **New Government Tenders and Procurement Law entering force (~120 days after Gazette publication) and the separate local-content/localisation framework.** Etimad process changes will follow; the SAR 1m direct-purchase ceiling matters for emergency and short-term environmental services.
5. **1 January 2027 — NCEC deadline to apply for fine instalments; NCEC Q4 enforcement report.** Expect a permit-application rush among lowest- and medium-impact facilities — a consultancy sales window.
6. **MWAN's 27 opportunities (SAR 2.1bn) — tender platform, deadlines and pre-qualification criteria,** especially the Dammam (13, SAR 973m) and Madinah (7, SAR 780m) packages with medical-waste plants; also whether any Makkah-region hazardous-waste slots appear.
7. **Red Sea front and the pact.** Houthi attack tempo around Yanbu/Rabigh/Jeddah after the 24 October alerts; Jazan refinery restart; Türkiye's ratification vote; any Hormuz corridor agreement under the Omani proposal.
8. **Year-end openings and awards.** Red Sea Global Phase One (27 hotels, Shura island), SPPC Round 7 awards and battery group 2 shortlist, Qiddiya District 0 infrastructure award, and whether Sharakat's Ras Mohaisen–Baha–Mecca transmission contract is confirmed for 2027.

### Big picture (six points)

1. **The war has become a cost, not a windfall.** Q2's smaller deficit was a one-off: in Q3 oil revenue fell 23% because the Kingdom could not ship enough, the nine-month deficit is ~148% of the full-year plan, and 2027 is budgeted at a SAR 190bn deficit. Government buyers will fund compliance and must-do infrastructure, not optional studies.
2. **Corporate Saudi Arabia is recovering faster than the state.** Aramco's cash again covers its dividend, Petro Rabigh and SABIC are back in profit, Acwa's profit jumped 71% — thanks to margins and rerouted logistics. EH's industrial clients have money; its government clients are stretched.
3. **The front line moved to EH's coast and corridor.** The East-West pipeline, Yanbu, Rabigh and even Aramco's Dhahran HQ were hit or targeted between September and October, and civil-defence alerts reached Jeddah. Spill-response, remediation and emergency-planning services are now a live, local need rather than a scenario.
4. **Hormuz is "open but dangerous"; Bab el-Mandeb is Houthi-controlled.** Oil flows are near pre-war levels through escorted corridors, but Iran keeps the strait formally closed and the Houthis hold the islands at the Red Sea's southern gate. Plan for a long period of elevated maritime risk with gradual Gulf-coast industrial restarts.
5. **Regulation is doing the market's work.** NCEC's 6,449 quarterly violations, SAR 5m marine-pollution fines, Riyadh dust enforcement and MWAN's priced SAR 2.1bn facility slate turn the "$112bn opportunity" narrative into enforceable, biddable demand — the strongest structural tailwind for EH's permitting, monitoring, medical-waste and treatment services.
6. **PIF's strategy is reshaping who EH competes with and partners with.** Waste and recycling (SIRC) now sit in a named PIF ecosystem beside Acwa, and PIF wants private operators to build; at the same time Veolia has signed its way into Acwa, Ma'aden and GASCO, and HUMAIN's data-centre build-out has Aramco's balance sheet behind it. EH's route to scale is as a regional partner and specialist subcontractor on these platforms — in Makkah, Madinah and the Red Sea corridor first.
